# Small Business Pricing Guide: Calculate Costs, Markup and Profit Margin

Setting a sustainable price starts with understanding the full cost of providing a product or service. A price that covers materials but ignores fees, labour, or overhead can leave a business with less profit than expected.

## List Your Costs

Separate costs that change with each sale from overheads that support the business. Examples include materials, packaging, delivery, payment fees, rent, software, labour, returns, and marketing. Allocate shared overheads consistently when estimating a product's cost.

## Markup and Margin Are Different

**Markup (%) = Profit ÷ Cost × 100**

**Profit Margin (%) = Profit ÷ Selling Price × 100**

If an item costs ₹800 and sells for ₹1,000, the gross difference is ₹200. Markup on cost is 25%, while margin on selling price is 20%.

## Build a Price Estimate

1. Estimate the full cost per unit.
2. Decide the target margin or markup.
3. Calculate a price that covers the target.
4. Compare it with customer demand and competitors.
5. Recheck after supplier prices, fees, or taxes change.

For a target margin, price is not simply cost plus the same percentage. For example, to target a 20% margin on a ₹800 cost, the price would be ₹800 ÷ (1 − 0.20) = ₹1,000, before any omitted costs.

## Avoid Common Pricing Mistakes

- Confusing revenue with profit.
- Ignoring payment, platform, delivery, and return costs.
- Applying discounts without checking the remaining margin.
- Using competitor prices without comparing product quality and service.
- Forgetting that tax treatment depends on the business and jurisdiction.

Use pricing calculations as estimates and keep them updated with real sales and expense records.

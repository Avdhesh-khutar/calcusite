# Study Target Planner: Use Marks Goals to Plan Exam Preparation

A study target planner helps you turn an exam goal into smaller, measurable steps. It cannot predict the score you will earn, but it can help you plan revision and track progress.

## Start With a Clear Target

Check the exam's maximum marks, your completed assessment scores, and the percentage or grade you are aiming for. Use the marks-needed calculation only if the course's assessment rules allow scores to be combined in that way.

## Divide the Remaining Work

1. List subjects and topics still to revise.
2. Identify topics with high exam weight or where you make frequent mistakes.
3. Divide available study sessions across the remaining days.
4. Reserve time for practice papers and reviewing errors.
5. Revisit the plan weekly based on progress.

## Use Practice Results Carefully

A practice test is a snapshot, not a guarantee of the final score. Review why an answer was wrong—concept gap, memory, calculation, or time management—and use that information to choose the next task.

## Make the Plan Realistic

Include breaks, sleep, school or work commitments, and time for difficult topics. A plan that cannot be followed is less useful than a smaller plan that you can sustain.

## Track Progress

Record practice scores and topics completed, but avoid judging your ability from one test. If a topic remains confusing, ask a teacher or classmate for help.

Use target calculations as planning aids, and check your school's official exam structure and grading rules.
